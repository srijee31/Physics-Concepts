# 28. Acceleration Due to Gravity (g)

## 📌 What is Acceleration Due to Gravity?

Acceleration due to gravity (g) is the **acceleration with which objects fall towards the Earth** due to gravitational force.

👉 It acts in the downward direction (towards the center of Earth)

---

## 📐 Value of g

g = 9.8 m/s² (on Earth)

👉 This means velocity increases by 9.8 m/s every second during free fall

---

## 🧠 Why does it happen?

Because Earth attracts objects with gravitational force.

👉 This force causes objects to accelerate while falling

---

## ⚡ Key Points

- g is constant near the surface of Earth  
- Direction of g is always downward  
- Independent of mass (all objects fall at same rate in vacuum)  

---

## 🔽 Variation of g

### 1. With Height
- As height increases → g decreases  

### 2. With Depth
- As depth increases → g decreases  

### 3. At Different Places
- g is slightly more at poles  
- g is slightly less at equator  

---

## 🔁 Free Fall

When an object falls only under gravity (no air resistance):

👉 It is called free fall  
👉 Acceleration = g  

---

## 📐 Equations of Motion (using g)

v = u + gt  
s = ut + (1/2)gt²  
v² = u² + 2gs  

---

## 🧠 Example Problem

An object falls from rest for 2 seconds.

u = 0, g = 9.8 m/s²  

v = 0 + (9.8 × 2)  
v = 19.6 m/s  

---

## 🧠 Summary

- g = acceleration due to gravity  
- Value = 9.8 m/s²  
- Acts downward  
- Same for all objects (in vacuum)  
- Used in motion equations  

---

## ✅ One-Line Definition

👉 Acceleration due to gravity is the acceleration of an object caused by Earth's gravitational pull.
