# 48. Thermal Expansion

## 📌 Definition
Thermal expansion is the increase in dimensions (length, area, or volume) of a material when its temperature rises.

---

## 🎯 Types of Thermal Expansion

### 1️⃣ Linear Expansion (Length)
Change in length of a solid when temperature changes.

ΔL = L₀ α ΔT

---

### 2️⃣ Area Expansion (Surface)
Change in area of a material.

ΔA = A₀ β ΔT

---

### 3️⃣ Volume Expansion (Cubical)
Change in volume of a substance.

ΔV = V₀ γ ΔT

---

## 🔗 Coefficient Relations (for solids)

- β = 2α  
- γ = 3α  

Where:
- α = coefficient of linear expansion  
- β = coefficient of area expansion  
- γ = coefficient of volume expansion  

---

## 📊 Terms Explained

- ΔL, ΔA, ΔV → Change in length, area, volume  
- L₀, A₀, V₀ → Original dimensions  
- ΔT → Change in temperature  
- α, β, γ → Expansion coefficients (material-dependent)

---

## ⚡ Key Points

- Expansion happens when temperature **increases**
- Contraction happens when temperature **decreases**
- Different materials expand at different rates
- Liquids and gases show **only volume expansion**

---

## 🌍 Real-Life Examples

- Railway tracks have gaps (to avoid bending)  
- Bridges use expansion joints  
- Electric wires sag in summer  
- Liquid in thermometer expands with heat  

---

## 🧠 Why does it happen?

When temperature increases:
- Particles gain kinetic energy  
- They vibrate more  
- Distance between particles increases  
👉 Result: Expansion  

---
