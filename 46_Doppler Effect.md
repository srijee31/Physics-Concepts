# 46. Doppler Effect

## 📌 Definition
The Doppler Effect is the change in frequency (or pitch) of a wave due to the relative motion between the source and the observer.

---

## 🚗 Real-Life Example
- When an ambulance approaches you → sound is **high pitch**
- When it moves away → sound becomes **low pitch**

---

## 🎯 Key Concept
- Source moving **towards observer** → Frequency increases (pitch ↑)
- Source moving **away from observer** → Frequency decreases (pitch ↓)

---

## ⚡ Reason
- Approaching source → Waves get **compressed** → Higher frequency  
- Receding source → Waves get **stretched** → Lower frequency  

---

## 📊 Formula

f' = f ((v ± v_o) / (v ∓ v_s))

Where:
- f' = observed frequency  
- f = actual frequency  
- v = speed of sound  
- v_o = velocity of observer  
- v_s = velocity of source  

---

## ⚠️ Sign Convention
- Use **+** when moving **towards**
- Use **−** when moving **away**

Trick:
- Observer velocity (v_o): take sign based on observer motion  
- Source velocity (v_s): take opposite sign of its motion  

---

## 🌍 Applications
- Ambulance and police sirens  
- Train horn  
- Radar speed guns  
- Astronomy (Redshift and Blueshift)  

---

## 💡 Quick Memory Trick
"Closer = Higher Pitch, Farther = Lower Pitch"
