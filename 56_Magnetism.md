# 56_Magnetism

## Introduction

Magnetism is a physical phenomenon produced by moving electric charges, certain materials, and magnetic fields. It is the force by which magnets attract or repel each other.

Magnets have two poles:

- North Pole
- South Pole

Like poles repel each other, and unlike poles attract each other.

---

## What is a Magnet?

A magnet is an object that can produce a magnetic field and attract magnetic materials such as:

- Iron
- Nickel
- Cobalt
- Steel

Examples:

- Bar magnet
- Horseshoe magnet
- Electromagnet

---

## Magnetic Field

A magnetic field is the region around a magnet where magnetic force can be felt.

It is represented by magnetic field lines.

### Properties of Magnetic Field Lines:

- They emerge from the north pole and enter the south pole.
- Inside the magnet, they move from south to north.
- They never intersect each other.
- Closer lines indicate stronger magnetic field.

---

## Types of Magnetic Materials

### 1. Ferromagnetic Materials

Strongly attracted by magnets.

Examples:

- Iron
- Nickel
- Cobalt

### 2. Paramagnetic Materials

Weakly attracted by magnets.

Examples:

- Aluminium
- Platinum

### 3. Diamagnetic Materials

Weakly repelled by magnets.

Examples:

- Copper
- Gold
- Silver

---

## Methods of Magnetization

Magnetic materials can be turned into magnets by:

- Stroking method
- Induction method
- Electric current method

---

## Electromagnet

An electromagnet is a temporary magnet produced by electric current flowing through a coil.

### Uses:

- Electric bell
- Cranes for lifting scrap iron
- Motors
- Relays

---

## Earth's Magnetism

The Earth behaves like a giant magnet.

- Geographic north is near magnetic south pole.
- A compass needle points north-south due to Earth's magnetic field.

---

## Magnetic Force

The force exerted by one magnet on another magnetic object or magnet is called magnetic force.

It can be:

- Attractive force
- Repulsive force

---

## Uses of Magnetism

- Compass navigation
- Electric motors
- Generators
- Speakers
- MRI machines
- Credit cards
- Magnetic storage devices

---

## Advantages of Magnetism

- Useful in machines
- Helps in navigation
- Used in medical devices
- Helps generate electricity

---

## Disadvantages of Magnetism

- Can damage electronic data
- Interference in devices
- Strong magnets may be dangerous

---

## Important Formulas

### Magnetic Force on Moving Charge

F = q(v × B)

Where:

- F = magnetic force
- q = charge
- v = velocity
- B = magnetic field

### Unit of Magnetic Field

Tesla (T)

---

## Key Points

- Every magnet has two poles.
- Like poles repel, unlike poles attract.
- Moving charges create magnetism.
- Earth itself is a magnet.
- Electromagnets work using electric current.

---

## Conclusion

Magnetism is an important branch of physics used in daily life and modern technology. From compasses to electric motors, magnetism plays a vital role in science and engineering.
