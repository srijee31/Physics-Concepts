# Law of Conservation of Energy

## Definition

The Law of Conservation of Energy states that **energy cannot be created or destroyed**, but it can only be **transformed** from one form to another. The total energy of an isolated system remains constant over time.

## Key Points

- Energy can be transferred between objects or converted between different forms (e.g., from kinetic energy to potential energy).
- In any process, the total amount of energy in a closed system is conserved.
- The principle applies to all types of energy, including mechanical, electrical, thermal, chemical, and nuclear energy.

## Forms of Energy

1. **Kinetic Energy (KE)**: Energy due to motion.
   - Formula: `KE = 1/2 mv^2`
   - Where: 
     - `m` = mass of the object
     - `v` = velocity of the object

2. **Potential Energy (PE)**: Energy due to position or configuration.
   - Formula: `PE = mgh`
   - Where:
     - `m` = mass of the object
     - `g` = acceleration due to gravity
     - `h` = height of the object

3. **Thermal Energy**: Energy related to the temperature of an object.

4. **Chemical Energy**: Stored energy in chemical bonds.

5. **Electrical Energy**: Energy due to the flow of electric charge.

6. **Nuclear Energy**: Energy stored in the nucleus of atoms.

## Examples

- **Pendulum**: A pendulum demonstrates the conversion between kinetic and potential energy. At the highest point, the pendulum has maximum potential energy and minimum kinetic energy. As it swings down, potential energy is converted into kinetic energy, and at the lowest point, the pendulum has maximum kinetic energy.
  
- **Roller Coaster**: In a roller coaster, at the top of the hill, the car has maximum potential energy. As it moves down, potential energy is converted into kinetic energy, causing the car to speed up.

## Applications

- **Mechanical Systems**: Understanding how energy is conserved helps in designing efficient machines and engines.
- **Electricity Generation**: In power plants, mechanical energy (from turbines) is converted to electrical energy, but the total energy remains conserved.

## Conclusion

The Law of Conservation of Energy is one of the fundamental principles of physics. It is universally applicable to all systems and is a key principle in understanding energy transfer, transformation, and efficiency in both natural and engineered systems.
