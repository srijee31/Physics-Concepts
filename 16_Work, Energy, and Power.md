# Physics Study Notes: Work, Energy, and Power

In physics, **Work** and **Energy** are fundamental concepts used to describe how forces interact with objects to cause motion or change. They are scalar quantities and are linked by the **Work-Energy Theorem**.

---

## 1. Work ($W$)
Work is done when a force acting on an object causes a displacement. If there is no movement, no physical work is done, regardless of the effort applied.

### Mathematical Definition
The work done by a constant force is the product of the component of the force in the direction of displacement and the magnitude of the displacement:

$$W = \vec{F} \cdot \vec{d} = F d \cos(\theta)$$

* **$F$**: Magnitude of the force (N).
* **$d$**: Magnitude of the displacement (m).
* **$\theta$**: The angle between the force vector and the displacement vector.



### Nature of Work
| Type | Condition | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Positive** | $0^\circ \leq \theta < 90^\circ$ | Pushing a car forward. |
| **Negative** | $90^\circ < \theta \leq 180^\circ$ | Friction acting against a sliding box. |
| **Zero** | $\theta = 90^\circ$ or $d = 0$ | Carrying a box horizontally (force is up, motion is sideways). |

---

## 2. Energy ($E$)
Energy is the **capacity to do work**. In mechanics, we focus on **Mechanical Energy**, which is the sum of kinetic and potential energy.

### A. Kinetic Energy ($K$)
The energy possessed by an object due to its **motion**.
$$K = \frac{1}{2}mv^2$$
* Always positive or zero.
* Measured in Joules (J).

### B. Potential Energy ($U$)
The energy stored in an object due to its **position** or configuration.
1.  **Gravitational Potential Energy**: $U_g = mgh$
    * $g \approx 9.8 \text{ m/s}^2$
2.  **Elastic Potential Energy**: $U_s = \frac{1}{2}kx^2$
    * Stored in compressed or stretched springs.



---

## 3. The Work-Energy Theorem
The net work done by all forces acting on an object is equal to the change in its kinetic energy.
$$W_{net} = \Delta K = K_f - K_i$$

---

## 4. Conservation of Energy
In an isolated system, energy can neither be created nor destroyed; it can only be transformed.

### Conservation of Mechanical Energy
If only **conservative forces** (like gravity) are doing work, the total mechanical energy remains constant:
$$(K + U)_{initial} = (K + U)_{final}$$

---

## 5. Power ($P$)
Power is the **rate** at which work is done or energy is transferred.
$$P = \frac{W}{t}$$
* **SI Unit**: Watt (W). $1 \text{ W} = 1 \text{ J/s}$.
* **Alternative Formula**: $P = \vec{F} \cdot \vec{v}$ (Force times velocity).
