## Impulse in Physics

### What is Impulse?
Impulse is the change in momentum of an object when a force acts upon it for an interval of time. It’s given by the formula:

\[
\text{Impulse} = F \times \Delta t
\]

Where:
- \( F \) is the force applied
- \( \Delta t \) is the time interval during which the force is applied

### Key Concept:
Impulse is directly related to momentum, and the **Impulse-Momentum Theorem** states that the change in momentum of an object is equal to the impulse applied to it.

\[
\Delta p = F \times \Delta t
\]

Where:
- \( \Delta p \) is the change in momentum of the object.

### Unit:
The SI unit of impulse is **N·s** (Newton-seconds), which is the same as the unit of momentum (kg·m/s).

### Example:
If a bat hits a ball, the force applied by the bat over the time the ball is in contact with the bat results in an impulse that changes the ball’s momentum.
