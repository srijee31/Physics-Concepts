# Newton’s Second Law of Motion

## 1. What is it?
Newton’s Second Law describes exactly how the velocity of an object changes when it is subjected to an external force. It defines the relationship between **Force**, **Mass**, and **Acceleration**.

## 2. The Formula
The law is most commonly written as:
$$F = m \times a$$

Where:
* **$F$** = Force (Measured in Newtons, N)
* **$m$** = Mass (Measured in kilograms, kg)
* **$a$** = Acceleration (Measured in $m/s^2$)



[Image of Newton's Second Law F=ma diagram]


## 3. Simple Meaning
The more mass an object has, the more force you need to make it move or speed up. If you push two different objects with the same amount of force, the lighter one will accelerate much faster.

## 4. Real-Life Examples
* **Pushing a Shopping Cart:** An empty cart is easy to accelerate. A cart full of groceries (more mass) requires much more force to reach the same speed.
* **Cricket:** A professional bowler exerts a large force on a small cricket ball to make it accelerate to high speeds.
* **Car Engines:** A heavy truck needs a much more powerful engine (more force) than a small bike to start moving at a green light.

## 5. Why is it used?
* To calculate exactly how much fuel a rocket needs to reach space.
* To design car brakes that can stop a vehicle safely based on its weight.
* To understand how much force a motor needs to move a mechanical part.

## 6. Key Points
* **Direct Relation:** If you double the force, the acceleration doubles.
* **Inverse Relation:** If you double the mass, the acceleration is cut in half (for the same force).
* **Direction:** The object always accelerates in the same direction as the force applied.
* **Force is a Vector:** It has both a size (magnitude) and a specific direction.

### 📝 Quick Calculation
If a ball has a mass of **2 kg** and you push it with a force of **10 N**, what is the acceleration?
* **Calculation:** $a = F / m$  →  $10 / 2 = 5$
* **Result:** $5 m/s^2$
