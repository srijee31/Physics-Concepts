# 44. Sound Waves

## Definition
Sound waves are **mechanical longitudinal waves** produced by vibrating objects and transmitted through a material medium such as air, water, or solids.

They travel by causing particles of the medium to vibrate back and forth in the same direction as the wave travels.

## Key Characteristics of Sound Waves

### 1. Mechanical Wave
- Sound requires a **medium** to travel.
- It cannot travel in a vacuum.

### 2. Longitudinal Wave
- Particles vibrate parallel to the direction of wave motion.

### 3. Produced by Vibrations
- Example: Guitar string, speaker, drum, vocal cords.

## Terms Related to Sound Waves

### Compression
Region where particles are close together and pressure is high.

### Rarefaction
Region where particles are spread apart and pressure is low.

### Wavelength (λ)
Distance between two consecutive compressions or rarefactions.

### Frequency (f)
Number of vibrations per second.

Unit: **Hertz (Hz)**

### Time Period (T)
Time taken for one vibration.

\[
T = \frac{1}{f}
\]

### Speed of Sound
Depends on medium and temperature.

\[
v = f\lambda
\]

Where:  
- \(v\) = velocity of sound  
- \(f\) = frequency  
- \(\lambda\) = wavelength

## Speed of Sound in Different Media

| Medium | Approx Speed |
|--------|--------------|
| Air (20°C) | 343 m/s |
| Water | 1500 m/s |
| Steel | 5000 m/s |

Sound travels fastest in solids and slowest in gases.

## Audible Range

| Type | Frequency Range |
|------|-----------------|
| Infrasonic | Below 20 Hz |
| Audible Sound | 20 Hz – 20,000 Hz |
| Ultrasonic | Above 20,000 Hz |

## Properties of Sound

### Pitch
Depends on frequency.

### Loudness
Depends on amplitude.

### Quality / Timbre
Helps distinguish sounds from different sources.

## Reflection of Sound
When sound strikes a surface and bounces back.

Example: Echo

## Applications of Sound Waves
- SONAR
- Medical ultrasound
- Communication
- Music systems
- Noise detection

## Important Points
- Sound cannot travel in space.
- Sound travels in all directions from source.
- Higher frequency = higher pitch.
- Greater amplitude = louder sound.

## Real-Life Example
When a bell rings, it vibrates and creates compressions and rarefactions in air, which reach our ears as sound.
