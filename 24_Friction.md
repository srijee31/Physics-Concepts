## Friction in Physics

### What is Friction?
Friction is a force that resists the relative motion or tendency of such motion of two surfaces in contact. It acts in the opposite direction of motion.

### Types of Friction:
1. **Static Friction**: The frictional force that resists the initiation of motion between two surfaces.
2. **Kinetic (Dynamic) Friction**: The frictional force that opposes the motion once the object is already moving.
   - **Sliding Friction**: Occurs when two solid surfaces slide past each other.
   - **Rolling Friction**: Occurs when an object rolls over a surface.

### Frictional Force Formula:
The force of friction can be expressed as:

\[
f = \mu \times N
\]

Where:
- f is the frictional force
- \mu is the coefficient of friction (static or kinetic)
- N is the normal force (force perpendicular to the surface)

### Coefficient of Friction:
- The **coefficient of static friction** (\mu_s) is usually higher than the **coefficient of kinetic friction** (\mu_k) for the same surfaces.
- It depends on the nature of the surfaces in contact.
