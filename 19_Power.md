## Power: Concepts and Notes

In physics, **Power** is a measure of how quickly work is done or how fast energy is transferred. Here is a breakdown of the key concepts for your notes.

---

### 1. Definition
**Power (P)** is defined as the rate of doing work or the rate at which energy is consumed.
* If a person or machine does more work in less time, it has more power.
* If it takes longer to do the same amount of work, it has less power.

### 2. Formula
The basic formula for power is:

$$P = \frac{W}{t}$$

Where:
* **$P$** = Power
* **$W$** = Work Done (measured in Joules)
* **$t$** = Time taken (measured in seconds)

Since Work Done is also equal to Energy Consumed, it can also be written as:
$$P = \frac{E}{t}$$

### 3. Units of Power
* **SI Unit:** The standard unit is the **Watt (W)**.
* **Definition of 1 Watt:** 1 Watt is the power of an agent which does work at the rate of 1 Joule per second ($1 W = 1 J/s$).
* **Larger Units:** * 1 Kilowatt (kW) = 1,000 Watts
    * 1 Megawatt (MW) = 1,000,000 Watts
    * 1 Horsepower (hp) = 746 Watts (commonly used for engines)

### 4. Average Power
When the rate of doing work varies over time, we calculate **Average Power**:
$$\text{Average Power} = \frac{\text{Total Work Done}}{\text{Total Time Taken}}$$

### 5. Commercial Unit of Energy
While the Joule is the SI unit, it is too small for commercial use (like your electricity bill). Instead, we use the **Kilowatt-hour (kWh)**.
* **1 kWh** is the energy consumed by an appliance of 1 kW power in 1 hour.
* **Conversion:** $1 \text{ kWh} = 3.6 \times 10^6 \text{ Joules}$
