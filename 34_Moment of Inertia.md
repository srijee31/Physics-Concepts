# 34. Moment of Inertia

## 📌 What is Moment of Inertia?

Moment of Inertia (I) is the **resistance of an object to rotational motion**.

👉 It is the rotational equivalent of mass  

---

## 🧠 Simple Understanding

- Mass → resists linear motion  
- Moment of Inertia → resists rotation  

👉 More I = harder to rotate  

---

## 📐 Formula

I = m × r²

Where:
- I = moment of inertia  
- m = mass  
- r = distance from axis of rotation  

---

## ⚡ Key Concept

👉 Depends on:
- Mass of the object  
- Distance of mass from axis  

👉 Mass farther from axis → higher inertia  

---

## 🔥 Real-Life Examples

- 🧍 Person spinning with arms open vs closed  
- 🎡 Ferris wheel (large radius → large inertia)  
- 🛞 Bicycle wheel  

👉 Spreading mass outward makes rotation harder  

---

## ⚡ Important Points

- Greater distance → greater moment of inertia  
- Shape of object affects I  
- Not constant for all objects  

---

## 🔁 Relation with Torque

τ = I × α  

Where:
- τ = torque  
- I = moment of inertia  
- α = angular acceleration  

👉 Same as F = ma in linear motion  

---

## 🧠 Example Problem

A mass of 2 kg is at distance 3 m from axis.

I = 2 × (3²)  
I = 2 × 9  
I = 18 kg·m²  

---

## 🧠 Summary

- Moment of inertia = resistance to rotation  
- Formula: I = mr²  
- Depends on mass and distance  
- Unit = kg·m²  

---

## ✅ One-Line Definition

👉 Moment of inertia is the resistance of an object to rotational motion about an axis.
