# 30. Escape Velocity

## 📌 What is Escape Velocity?

Escape Velocity is the **minimum velocity required for an object to escape the gravitational pull of a planet** without coming back.

👉 After reaching this velocity, the object will not fall back to Earth

---

## 🧠 Simple Understanding

- Earth pulls everything downward  
- To escape this pull, an object needs enough speed  

👉 That minimum speed = Escape Velocity  

---

## 📐 Formula

vₑ = √(2GM / R)

Where:
- vₑ = escape velocity  
- G = gravitational constant  
- M = mass of the planet  
- R = radius of the planet  

---

## 🌍 Escape Velocity of Earth

vₑ = 11.2 km/s  

👉 ≈ 11200 m/s  

---

## ⚡ Key Points

- Does not depend on mass of the object  
- Depends on planet’s mass and radius  
- Greater planet mass → higher escape velocity  
- Smaller radius → higher escape velocity  

---

## 🚀 Real-Life Examples

- Rockets escaping Earth  
- Satellites launched into space  
- Space missions  

---

## ❗ Important Notes

- Air resistance is ignored  
- Once escape velocity is reached, no extra fuel is needed (ideal case)  

---

## 🧠 Example Understanding

If a rocket is launched with speed less than 11.2 km/s:

👉 It will come back to Earth  

If speed ≥ 11.2 km/s:

👉 It escapes Earth’s gravity  

---

## 🧠 Summary

- Escape velocity = minimum speed to leave planet  
- Formula: vₑ = √(2GM / R)  
- Earth value = 11.2 km/s  
- Independent of object’s mass  

---

## ✅ One-Line Definition

👉 Escape velocity is the minimum velocity required for an object to escape a planet's gravitational field.
